# Emerging Markets & India

Adjustments for markets where accounting is noisier, governance varies, and the macro backdrop
matters more. Load as an **overlay on any path** when analyzing an EM or India name.

## 1. Levine & Zervos (1998) — Stock Market Development and Long-Run Growth

**Mechanism:** Stock-market development (liquidity, size, integration) is robustly correlated with
long-run economic growth. Markets both reflect and enable development. The macro lens for building a
long-term EM thesis — the rising-tide context that lifts well-positioned names.

**Application checklist:**
- Anchor an EM thesis in the development trajectory: deepening capital markets, financial inclusion,
  formalization, rising liquidity.
- Identify which businesses are levered to that structural deepening (financials, exchanges,
  consumer formalization, infrastructure).
- Separate structural development tailwind from cyclical macro noise.

**What it changes:** Provides the top-down justification for a multi-year EM allocation and
identifies the structurally advantaged sectors.

**Failure modes:**
- Confusing a cyclical macro bounce with structural development.
- Assuming market development lifts all boats equally — it concentrates in enablers.

## 2. Bhattacharya, Daouk & Welker (2003) — Earnings Quality in Emerging Markets

**Mechanism:** Reported earnings in EMs are **noisier and more opaque** — more earnings smoothing,
more aggressive recognition, weaker enforcement. Higher "earnings opacity" raises the cost of equity
and depresses valuations at the market level.

**Application checklist:**
- Apply a **higher skepticism bar** to EM accruals (links to Sloan in accounting-quality.md) —
  the accrual signal is noisier but the manipulation risk is higher.
- Adjust models for opacity: wider sensitivity ranges, haircut to reported margins where disclosure is thin.
- Reward genuine transparency: EM firms with clean, conservative, IFRS/US-GAAP-grade disclosure
  deserve a relative premium.

**What it changes:** Recalibrates earnings-quality thresholds upward and justifies an opacity
discount (or a transparency premium) in EM valuations.

**Failure modes:**
- Applying developed-market accrual benchmarks unadjusted to EM names.
- Treating thin disclosure as neutral rather than as a priced risk.

## 3. Durnev & Kim (2005) — Corporate Governance and Firm Valuation

**Mechanism:** In EMs, firm-level governance varies widely and is **priced** — better-governed firms
trade at a valuation premium, and the premium is larger where legal protection is weaker. Governance
is a bigger valuation swing factor in EMs than in developed markets. Explains the conglomerate
discount in promoter-led groups.

**Application checklist:**
- Score firm-level governance (disclosure quality, board independence, minority protection, capital
  discipline) and size the premium/discount vs. EM peers.
- Promoter-led conglomerates: quantify the governance/holding-company discount and judge whether a
  catalyst (restructuring, improved disclosure, minority-friendly action) could close it.
- Combine with Tunneling (corporate-governance.md): the discount is rational where extraction risk is real.

**What it changes:** Turns governance from a qualitative footnote into a sized valuation
input — often the decisive variable for an EM promoter-led name.

**Failure modes:**
- Buying a "cheap" conglomerate without recognizing the discount is rational governance pricing.
- Assuming a governance re-rating without a concrete catalyst.

## Synthesis (EM overlay)

The EM overlay modifies three things on top of whatever path is running: **raise the earnings-quality
bar (Bhattacharya)**, **size the governance premium/discount explicitly (Durnev-Kim, with Tunneling)**,
and **anchor the long thesis in structural development, not cyclical noise (Levine-Zervos)**. For
promoter-led names the governance and tunneling layers are usually decisive — a low multiple is often
rational, not an opportunity, unless a specific catalyst closes the gap.

> **INDIA / STANDARDS NOTE:** Reconcile the accounting basis before comparing. Indian issuers report
> under Ind-AS (broadly IFRS-converged but not identical); many EM peers use local GAAP or IFRS.
> Differences in revenue recognition, lease treatment, related-party disclosure, and consolidation of
> promoter group entities can distort cross-border comparisons. Normalize to a common basis or flag
> the gap before drawing a quality or valuation conclusion. Promoter pledging and group-company
> related-party flows are the highest-yield tunneling checks for Indian names.
