# Valuation Foundations

The papers that define what "intrinsic value" means and why active research can have value at all.
Load for **Path 5 (Valuation Integrity Check)** and the opening of **Path 6**.

## Contents
1. Williams (1938) — Theory of Investment Value
2. Miller & Modigliani (1961) — Dividend Policy, Growth, Valuation
3. Grossman & Stiglitz (1980) — Impossibility of Efficient Markets
4. Fama (1970) — Efficient Capital Markets

## 1. Williams (1938) — The Theory of Investment Value

**Mechanism:** The value of any asset is the present value of the cash it returns to its owners over
its life. For equities, that is discounted future dividends (broadly: all distributions to
shareholders). Every DCF, DDM, and FCFE model is a descendant of this one idea.

**Application checklist:**
- Trace the valuation back to actual cash reaching shareholders. Buybacks, dividends, eventual
  liquidation value — not accounting earnings abstractions.
- If a model values "earnings" or "EBITDA" without a bridge to distributable cash, it has drifted
  from first principles. Rebuild the bridge.
- Reinvested cash only creates value if it earns above the cost of capital. Otherwise it is
  destroyed, not stored (links directly to Jensen 1986).

**What it changes:** Forces every valuation to answer "cash to whom, and when." Kills models that
capitalize non-cash earnings.

**Failure modes:**
- Treating retained earnings as automatically value-additive.
- Valuing a company that never plausibly returns cash to minority holders (links to Tunneling).

## 2. Miller & Modigliani (1961) — Dividend Policy, Growth, and the Valuation of Shares

**Mechanism:** Under idealized conditions, dividend policy is irrelevant to value; what matters is
the earnings power of assets and the return on reinvestment. The practical legacy for an analyst:
**terminal value assumptions dominate equity valuations**, because most of the value sits in the
distant, reinvestment-driven future.

**Application checklist:**
- Interrogate terminal value first, not last. It is where the model lives or dies.
- Separate value from reinvestment policy: a growth assumption is only worth anything if ROIC > WACC.
- "Growth" with returns at or below the cost of capital adds zero value — model it as such.

**What it changes:** Reorders the audit. Terminal assumptions get the hardest scrutiny because they
carry the most weight.

**Failure modes:**
- Rewarding growth that does not clear the cost of capital.
- Letting a high terminal growth rate smuggle in value the operating model cannot justify.

## 3. Grossman & Stiglitz (1980) — On the Impossibility of Informationally Efficient Markets

**Mechanism:** If prices perfectly reflected all information, no one would be paid to gather
information, so no one would — and prices would stop reflecting it. Therefore markets *must* be
inefficient enough to compensate informed research. This is the theoretical license for the entire
job of equity analysis.

**Application checklist:**
- For every thesis, write one sentence: *what specifically does the market not yet know or
  misprice, and why hasn't it corrected?*
- If you cannot name the inefficiency, you do not have an edge — you have a consensus position with
  extra steps.
- Identify why the gap persists: information cost, complexity, time-horizon arbitrage, structural
  neglect (small-cap, EM, post-spin).

**What it changes:** Converts "I like this stock" into "here is the specific information advantage
and why it is not yet priced." This is the single most important discipline in Path 6.

**Failure modes:**
- Claiming an edge that is actually well-known consensus.
- Assuming an inefficiency exists without explaining its persistence.

## 4. Fama (1970) — Efficient Capital Markets: A Review of Empirical Work

**Mechanism:** The canonical EMH statement, in three forms — weak (prices reflect past prices),
semi-strong (prices reflect all public info), strong (prices reflect all info including private).
Every active strategy is implicitly a bet that one of these forms fails.

**Application checklist:**
- Name which form your thesis bets against:
  - Weak-form bet → you are using price/momentum patterns (links to Jegadeesh-Titman).
  - Semi-strong bet → you believe public info is mispriced (most fundamental research lives here).
  - Strong-form bet → you have, or infer, private information (legal edge: expert networks, channel checks).
- A defensible thesis specifies the form and the reason it fails for this name.

**What it changes:** Disciplines the edge claim into a precise, defensible category.

**Failure modes:**
- Running a semi-strong fundamental thesis while secretly relying on price action — mixing efficiency bets without noticing.
- Treating EMH as either fully true or fully false; it is a spectrum that varies by name and market.

---

## Synthesis for Path 5

The four papers form one chain: **value is discounted cash (Williams)**, **terminal/reinvestment
assumptions dominate that value (M&M)**, **realizing the gap requires the market to be wrong
(Grossman-Stiglitz)**, **in a specifiable way (Fama)**. A valuation that survives all four is
defensible at IC. One that skips the efficiency question is just arithmetic.
