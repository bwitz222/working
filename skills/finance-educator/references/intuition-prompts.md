# Intuition Prompt Library

## How to Run One

**Setup (1 min):** State the scenario. Give all facts needed. Be explicit about what you're asking.
**Think time (2–3 min):** Silence. Don't fill it.
**Collect views (2 min):** 3–4 different answers on the board. No judgment yet.
**Debrief (3–5 min):** Validate correct reasoning in wrong answers. Identify the flaw in incorrect ones. Who was right is secondary to why.

Never skip the debrief to stay on schedule. The debrief is where the learning happens.

## Equities

**The Buyback Signal**
"Company A and Company B: same sector, same size, same growth rate. A buys back 5% of shares per year. B pays a 5% dividend. You can only own one. Which, and why?"
*Targets: tax efficiency, signaling theory, capital allocation discipline*

**The P/E Trap**
"Stock A has a P/E of 10. Stock B has a P/E of 35. A PM tells you A is obviously cheaper. Is she right?"
*Targets: PEG ratio, growth vs. value, earnings quality, CAPE*

**The Analyst Downgrade**
"An analyst downgrades a stock from Buy to Hold. The stock immediately jumps 4%. What just happened?"
*Targets: information already priced in, short covering, sentiment vs. fundamentals*

**The Profitable Decline**
"A company's revenue is falling 8% per year. Its stock is up 40% this year. First hypothesis?"
*Targets: multiple expansion, buybacks, margin improvement on lower revenue, sector rotation*

## Fixed Income

**The 2022 Bond Massacre**
"In 2022, a pension fund held 30-year US Treasuries — the safest assets in the world. By year end, that portfolio was down 35%. Walk me through how that happened."
*Targets: duration risk, price-yield relationship, mark-to-market vs. hold-to-maturity*

**The Yield Curve Signal**
"The 2-year Treasury yields 5.2%. The 10-year yields 4.1%. What is the bond market telling you about the economy? What would you do with a corporate bond portfolio right now?"
*Targets: inverted yield curve, recession signal, credit spread behavior in slowdowns*

**The Credit Spread Puzzle**
"High-yield corporates yield 7.5% vs. 4.5% for Treasuries — a spread of 300bps. Wide or tight? What would you need to know to form a view?"
*Targets: historical spread context, what widening signals, implied default probabilities*

**The Negative Rate World**
"In 2020, Swiss 10-year government bonds yielded -0.5%. Guaranteed to lose money if held to maturity. Who buys this, and why?"
*Targets: currency appreciation speculation, regulatory capital requirements, deflation protection, forced institutional buyers*

## Portfolio Construction

**The 60/40 Autopsy**
"In 2022, a classic 60/40 portfolio fell roughly 17%. The whole point of the bond allocation was to protect against equity drawdowns. What went wrong? Does 60/40 still make sense?"
*Targets: correlation breakdown in inflationary regimes, when bonds hedge equities and when they don't*

**The Concentrated Bet**
"A PM puts 40% of the portfolio in one stock — Nvidia. Over two years, it returns 600%. He's the top performer in his category. Was it a good decision?"
*Targets: outcome vs. process, survivorship bias, Kelly criterion, risk management divorced from returns*

**The Rebalancing Discipline**
"Your portfolio started 60/40. After the equity rally, it's now 75/25. Your model says sell equities and rebalance. But equities are still going up. Do you rebalance?"
*Targets: rebalancing discipline, why it forces buy-low/sell-high over time, behavioral finance*

**The Hedge That Hurt**
"A PM buys put options to hedge a 20% drawdown. Over three years, the market goes up 45% and the puts expire worthless. Was the hedge a mistake?"
*Targets: insurance cost vs. benefit, hedging as risk management not return enhancement, portfolio intent*

## Macro / Behavioral

**The Rate Hike Paradox**
"The Fed just raised rates 25bps. The stock market rallied 1.5%. What's the market telling us?"
*Targets: buy-the-news mechanics, forward guidance, relief rally when hike was smaller than feared*

**The Inflation Illusion**
"Your equity portfolio is up 8% this year. CPI was 6%. Did you make money?"
*Targets: real vs. nominal returns, inflation's impact on purchasing power, why TIPS exist*

**The Crowded Trade**
"In 2021, nearly every major hedge fund held the same five tech stocks. Same thesis. What's the risk — beyond the obvious?"
*Targets: crowding risk, liquidity in drawdowns, correlation of supposedly uncorrelated strategies, forced liquidation*

**The Central Bank Put**
"Markets recover quickly after crashes partly because investors believe the Fed will cut if things get bad enough — the 'Fed put.' Is this rational? What happens when the Fed can't cut?"
*Targets: moral hazard, policy constraints in inflationary environments, what changes when the backstop disappears*

## Metacognitive Prompts

Use at the end of a session or after any discussion where students have taken positions.

**What would change your mind?**
"You've argued X. What would you need to see in the market to conclude you were wrong? What's your falsification condition?"

**What's missing from this analysis?**
Show a simplified DCF or valuation. "If this were a real investment decision and you had 30 minutes to stress-test this model, what do you check first?"

**Who's on the other side?**
After any investment thesis: "You want to buy this asset. Someone else is selling it to you. Who is that person, and why do they think you're wrong?"

**What's the base rate?**
When students propose a bullish or bearish scenario: "How often, historically, does this scenario play out the way you're describing? Is your view more confident than the base rate justifies?"

## Level Calibration

**Finance 101:** Market Scenario and Historical Inquest. Make the setup concrete and specific. Define basis points. Don't assume familiarity with any instrument.

**Upper-level undergrads:** Add Devil's Advocate and metacognitive prompts. Push them to falsify their own views.

**Graduate:** All formats work. Increase setup complexity, give partial information, ask them to identify what's missing before forming a view.
