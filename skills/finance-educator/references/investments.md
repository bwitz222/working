# Investments Reference

## Topic Anchors

For each topic, use the listed anchor as the session hook. Real data only — the year, magnitude, and company matter.

| Topic | Anchor |
|---|---|
| Equities: valuation multiples | Tesla P/E vs. Ford P/E (2021–2022 re-rating); meme stocks; any sector de-rating |
| Equities: earnings and price | Pick any recent earnings miss or beat. Walk through what happened to the stock and why the market reacted as it did |
| Efficient markets / factors | SPIVA data: % of active managers underperforming the S&P 500 over 15 years (typically 90%+) |
| Bond pricing / duration | TLT ETF: -31% in 2022 as 10Y yields moved 1.5% → 4.2% |
| Yield curve | 2019 inversion followed by recession; 2023 uninversion; use FRED data |
| Credit spreads | HY spread widening March 2020 (crisis); tightening post-QE. ICE BofA index via FRED |
| Diversification | 60/40 in 2022: equities and bonds fell simultaneously — correlation spiked to 1 |
| Risk-adjusted returns | Compare a fund with high raw return but low Sharpe to a boring index fund with better Sharpe |
| Rebalancing | Show what a 60/40 portfolio looks like after 10 years without rebalancing — it becomes 80/20+ |
| Options basics | Earnings play where stock moves 10% but the option buyer loses money due to IV crush |
| Hedging vs. speculation | Southwest Airlines fuel hedges saved them in 2008; contrast with speculative oil positions |
| Futures | April 2020: WTI crude futures went negative (-$37/barrel). Memorable, vivid, real |
| Inflation and asset classes | TIPS vs. nominal bonds; real estate in 1970s stagflation; gold performance |
| Central bank policy | Map Fed rate decisions to SPX reactions 2022–2023. Show "don't fight the Fed" empirically |
| International diversification | EM vs. S&P 500 over 20 years; currency risk via 2015 China devaluation |

## Common Misconceptions — Address Proactively

**"Diversification always protects you."**
It doesn't when correlations spike toward 1. Show 2008 and 2022. True diversification needs negative or genuinely uncorrelated assets — not just more equities.

**"Higher return = better investment."**
A 20% return with Sharpe 0.3 is worse than 12% with Sharpe 1.1. Teach Sharpe before students calcify the wrong intuition.

**"Bonds are safe."**
Safe from default (Treasuries) ≠ safe from price risk. TLT's 2022 drawdown makes this visceral. Show the chart.

**"The market is always right."**
Grossman-Stiglitz paradox: markets are efficient only because people trade on information. If perfectly efficient, no one would gather information. The paradox destroys naive EMH and opens the door to factor investing.

**"You can time the market."**
Don't argue — show the data. Missing the 10 best days in the S&P 500 over 20 years destroys almost any timing strategy. Let the math close the argument.

## Mental Models Worth Teaching Explicitly

**Risk/Return Tradeoff** — Every investment decision is a tradeoff. Train students to ask "what am I being paid for this risk?" before evaluating any return number.

**Discount Rate as Opportunity Cost** — The discount rate is what you're giving up by not taking the next-best alternative. This reframe makes DCF intuitive rather than mechanical.

**Mr. Market** — Buffett/Graham's irrational business partner offering daily prices. Teaches that price ≠ value without requiring a lecture on behavioral finance.

**Kelly Criterion (conceptual)** — Size positions by edge × odds, not conviction alone. Students who internalize this don't blow up portfolios chasing concentrated bets.

## Free, Citable Data Sources

| Source | What it's good for |
|---|---|
| FRED (fred.stlouisfed.org) | Fed Funds rate, CPI, yield curves, HY spreads, unemployment |
| Yahoo Finance / Macrotrends | Historical stock and ETF price charts, downloadable |
| SPIVA (S&P) | Active vs. passive performance, updated annually |
| ICE BofA via FRED | Investment grade and high yield spread series |
| CME FedWatch | Market-implied Fed rate probabilities |
| Finviz / Koyfin | Sector performance, screeners, comparative data (free tier) |

## Content Hierarchy for Any Investments Session

Build every session in this order:
1. The market event or problem (why does this matter right now?)
2. The concept that explains it (the framework, introduced to solve the problem)
3. The mechanics (math/formula — minimal; only what's necessary to apply the concept)
4. The failure modes (when does this framework break? when would you not use it?)
5. Student intuition challenge (apply to an unseen situation)

Starting with mechanics before students care about the problem is the most common teaching failure in finance. Never invert this order.
