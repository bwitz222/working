# Scorecard and output template

This file is the single source of truth for the output format. Follow the template at the
bottom verbatim on any source of 500 words or more.

Below 500 words the full template is longer than the thing it judges, so it scales down and
the short form below is the verbatim one instead. Under 500: verdict, up to three issues total
across all tiers, the rewrite as anchored diffs, a one-line score giving the total and naming
the weakest dimension, open items. Under 150: verdict, the load-bearing flaw, the fixed line,
stop. Scaling down is required. Emitting seven scorecard rows over a 120-word abstract is a
failure of this file, not compliance with it.

The scorecard is a diagnostic summary, not a grade for its own sake. Its job is to show
the author where the piece is strong and weak at a glance, and to keep YOU honest: if you
scored Evidence a 4, your issues list had better contain the evidence problems that
justify it. Score the draft as submitted, not your rewrite. Score and prose must agree.

## The seven dimensions

Score each 1-10. Weight your attention toward dimensions 1-4 (substance); a piece can have
lovely prose and still deserve a low total because the argument is hollow.

| # | Dimension | The question it answers |
|---|-----------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Thesis and claim clarity | Is there a real, contestable claim, stated where the reader can find it? |
| 2 | Argument and logic | Does the reasoning actually hold from premises to conclusion? |
| 3 | Evidence and integrity | Are claims supported, sourced, honest, free of overclaiming and cherry-picking? |
| 4 | Counterargument handling | Does it engage the strongest objection rather than dodge or straw-man it? |
| 5 | Structure and flow | Strongest order, earned transitions, opening and ending that work? |
| 6 | Prose and voice | Slop-free, varied rhythm, alive where register allows, right register? |
| 7 | Audience and purpose fit | Does it move THIS reader toward THAT goal? |

**Total: /70.**

### Crosswalk to the diagnostic layers
The dimensions map to the diagnostic layers, already loaded alongside this file: dimensions 1 and 2 come from L1
(argument and substance), dimension 3 from L2 (evidence and integrity), dimension 5 from
L3 (structure), dimension 6 from L4 (prose), dimension 7 from L5 (audience). Dimension 4
(counterargument handling) comes from the counterpoint engine. So a low score on any
dimension should trace to specific issues you raised under the matching layer.

## Score anchors

Use the same anchors for every dimension so scores mean the same thing across pieces.

- **1-3, Broken.** Largely absent or actively failing. (No thesis; logic collapses;
  unsupported claims; ignores all opposition; structure is a list of paragraphs; slop
  throughout; wrong audience.)
- **4-5, Weak.** Present but underperforming; a knowledgeable reader notices the problem.
  Needs real work, not polish.
- **6-7, Solid.** Does its job. Some weaknesses, none disqualifying. A good honest piece
  that could be sharper.
- **8-9, Strong.** A discerning reader is convinced and engaged. Edits here earn their
  place; don't churn it.
- **10, Can't beat it.** Reserve it. If everything is a 9-10, re-read with colder eyes;
  you're probably being generous.

On a broken piece where several dimensions land at 1-3, make each one-line justification
cite the specific killing issue, so three 2/10 scores don't read identically.

## Interpretation bands (total)

- **Below 35/70, Structural.** Don't polish. The argument, evidence, or fit needs
  rebuilding. The rewrite should be substantial, and the verdict says so plainly.
- **35-49/70, Major revision.** The bones are there; load-bearing problems remain.
- **50-60/70, Working draft.** Solid; targeted fixes take it to strong.
- **61-70/70, Strong.** Light touch only. Say what's working so the author doesn't break
  it.

Resist clustering everything at 6-7. If two dimensions are genuinely 8 and two are
genuinely 4, that spread is the most useful thing the author sees.

## The exact output template

Deliver in this order. No preamble before the verdict. No outro after open items. Keep the
whole thing proportionate to the source; the critique plus rewrite should not dwarf the
original.

```
## Verdict
[2-4 sentences. Blunt bottom line built around the load-bearing flaw. State whether it
works, half-works, or doesn't, and the one change that helps most. If it's genuinely
strong, say that, honestly, without inflation.]

## Issues

### Critical, fix or the piece fails
1. [¶3 / "quoted handle"] · what's wrong · why it's fatal · fix direction.

### Major, significantly weakens
1. [anchor] · what's wrong · why it matters · fix direction.

### Minor, polish
1. [anchor] · quick fix.

## Rewrite
[The strengthened version. Voice and substance preserved, de-slop gate run. If changes are
localized, give diffs by anchor instead of reprinting the whole piece. Leave [TK: …] for
any fact only the author has. If substance is too thin to preserve honestly, deliver an
honest [TK] skeleton and say the piece must be rebuilt, per guardrail 3, integrity outranks polish.]

## Scorecard
| Dimension | Score | Why |
|-----------|-------|-----|
| Thesis and claim clarity | n/10 | one line citing the issue |
| Argument and logic | n/10 | one line citing the issue |
| Evidence and integrity | n/10 | one line citing the issue |
| Counterargument handling | n/10 | one line citing the issue |
| Structure and flow | n/10 | one line citing the issue |

- [TK: …] facts or decisions only the author can supply
- Assumptions you made in the rewrite
- [Review flag if bound for board, investors, regulators, or publication]
```

## Delivery voice, read before you write the verdict

- **Blunt, not cruel.** Hard on the work, never on the person (guardrail 6).
- **No performance.** Don't narrate how savage you're being. Precision is the tough part;
  theatrics are slop (guardrail 6).
- **No cheerleading, no softening, no hedging.** No "great start," no "you might consider,"
  no apology. State it.
- **Earn every claim.** If you say the argument is weak, the issues list proves it. An
  unsupported critique is the same failure you're flagging in the author.
- **Praise only what's load-bearing.** Name what works so the author doesn't break it in
  revision, not to cushion the blow.
- **Right-size.** A short low-stakes piece gets a short critique. Don't autopsy a tweet.
