# Finance domain rules

Loads whenever a piece touches a ticker, a portfolio, a client, or a financial figure, in any mode.

## Contents
- Part 1: the compliance gate
- Part 2: finance-specific tells
- Part 3: investment and research craft

## Part 1: the compliance gate

These are blocking. The draft does not ship until every rule is satisfied or the violation is
written into the open-items block where the human can see it. A silent pass is a failure.
Applies to anything a client, prospect, committee, or student might read, including drafts
that "will get cleaned up later."

**No performance promises or guarantees, ever.** No "will return," no "should compound at,"
no "protects your downside." If the draft promises, rewrite to a conditional tied to a stated
assumption: what has to be true, and what happens if it isn't. If the promise is the whole
point of the sentence, cut the sentence.

**No specific buy or sell recommendation without balanced risk language in the same passage.**
Not in an appendix, not in a footer disclaimer. Same passage. If the draft says to buy, the
adjacent lines state what would make that wrong and what the loss looks like. If you cannot
source the risk side, downgrade the recommendation to an observation and flag it.

**Past performance framing must not imply future results.** Kill the implicit bridge: "has
delivered 12% annually" followed by a forward-looking sentence reads as a forecast. Separate
them, state the period explicitly, and attach the standard limitation in plain words. Rewrite
trend-as-destiny constructions ("continues to," "has consistently," "reliably") into what
happened, over what window, under what conditions.

**Every price, yield, rate, valuation, multiple, or economic figure carries an as-of date and
a named source.** Figures from memory are prohibited. If live data was not pulled, say so in
the text and label the number an estimate: "roughly 4.3%, estimate, not pulled live [TK:
confirm vs. source]." An unsourced number is not a small defect. It is the defect that ends
careers. Missing facts become a visible [TK: ...] placeholder, never a plausible guess.

**Anything reading as personalized investment advice gets flagged for human review before it
ships.** The trigger is second person plus specificity: "you should shift your allocation,"
"given your tax situation, sell." Flag it in the open-items block with the exact line quoted.
Do not quietly soften it into generic language, because that hides a decision the human needs
to make.

**Math that reaches a deliverable is verified programmatically, not eyeballed.** Run the
arithmetic in code: totals, weights summing to 100, CAGRs, basis point deltas, implied
multiples, allocation drift. Report that it was verified. If the math could not be run,
label every derived figure unverified and flag it.

**When a piece carries model output, route provenance through the decision-provenance skill.**
Any figure, ranking, screen result, or conclusion generated by a model needs a recorded trail
of what produced it and on what inputs. Provenance is part of the deliverable, not a
courtesy. If that skill is unavailable, record the inputs and the method inline in open
items and flag that provenance was captured manually. The rule is never satisfied by silence.

## Part 2: finance-specific tells

Beyond generic slop. These are the things that make finance writing bad even when the prose
is clean.

| Tell | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Consensus restated as insight | Cut it or name it as the base case, then state the variant view and why the market has it wrong. If there is no variant view, the piece has no reason to exist. Say so. |
| False precision on a modeled figure | Round to the precision the inputs support. A DCF output is not accurate to the cent. Give a range with the driver that moves it. |
| Hedge-everything analyst voice | One position per claim. Replace "may potentially face some headwinds" with the specific headwind, its size, and its timing. If genuinely uncertain, state the uncertainty as a probability or a range, not as fog. |
| Thesis that survives any outcome | Force falsifiability: name the observable that would prove it wrong, and by when. A thesis no evidence can kill is not a thesis. |
| "Well-positioned," "attractive risk/reward," "compelling entry point" | Attach numbers or delete. Positioned relative to whom, on what metric? Attractive at what price, against what downside, over what horizon? |
| Valuation conclusion with no stated driver | Name the one or two variables doing the work: margin path, terminal growth, multiple rerating, rate assumption. Show the sensitivity. |
| Risk section listing generic market risk | Replace with risks specific to this thesis: the assumption most likely wrong, the concentration, the customer, the covenant, the regulatory decision. "Market volatility" is filler that signals no one thought about it. |
| Adjectives standing in for evidence | "Strong," "robust," "healthy," "significant" carry no data. Substitute the number, the comparison, or the trend that earned the adjective. |
| Precision theater in the summary, vagueness in the body | The number in the headline must trace to a line in the analysis. If it doesn't, one of them is wrong. |
| Backward-looking narrative dressed as a forecast | Separate what happened from what you expect. Mark the transition explicitly. |

## Part 3: investment and research craft

Investment memos, research notes, theses, IC decks, market commentary, LP letters, client
reviews. The reader is a skeptical professional with capital at risk. They are looking for
reasons to say no.

**Demand a variant view.** What does this say that consensus doesn't? A note that restates
the sell-side narrative has no reason to exist. If there's no differentiated view, that's the
load-bearing flaw. Name it.

**Edge must be explicit.** Why does this opportunity exist and why hasn't it been arbitraged
away? "Cheap on P/E" is not a thesis. If the author can't say why the market is wrong, the
idea is probably consensus or a value trap.

**Base rates over stories.** A vivid narrative ("the next Nvidia") that ignores how rarely
such outcomes occur is base-rate neglect. Demand the reference class: how often do companies
like this do what's claimed?

**The bear case is mandatory and must be steelmanned.** A thesis with no genuine
counter-thesis, or a straw-man bear case, is a critical flaw. The strongest reason a smart
investor passes belongs in the memo, answered. Pre-mortem the position: it's down 40% in a
year, what did the author miss?

**Risk and downside, not just upside.** What's the drawdown, the failure mode, the position
sizing, what kills the thesis? Upside-only is a tell of motivated reasoning.

**Data integrity.** Cherry-picked start dates, survivorship in backtests, returns without
risk adjustment (Sharpe, max drawdown, vol), false precision in projections, single data
points used as proof. Any of these can be critical for an external reader.

**Distinguish levels.** Is the claim about the business, the security, or the trade? "Great
company" and "good investment at this price" are different claims; conflating them is a
common, serious error.

**Time horizon and catalyst.** Why now, and what makes the gap close? A correct thesis with
no catalyst and no horizon is unactionable.

**Teaching carries the same evidentiary bar.** A worked example built on invented numbers
teaches students to invent numbers. Label illustrative figures as illustrative, and keep real
ones sourced and dated like anything else.
