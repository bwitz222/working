# Craft: Cross-Cutting Techniques

Read this when a draft needs structural or stylistic work beyond the de-slop gate.
Form-agnostic; pairs with whichever fork is active.

## Openings
The first sentence sells the second. Strong starts: a concrete image, a specific
claim, a number that surprises, a tension, a scene mid-motion. Weak starts:
throat-clearing context, dictionary definitions, "In today's…," "Imagine a…,"
rhetorical questions you immediately answer. If the open feels generic, cut the
first paragraph and start at the second. Often the real opening was hiding there.

## Transitions
Transitions should carry logic, not decorate it. Prefer the small connectives that
show relationship ("but," "so," "because," "which means," "even so") over the
formal blanks ("Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," "In addition"). The best
transition is often none: two paragraphs in logical sequence don't need a bridge
word. Echo a key noun from the prior paragraph to thread continuity.

## Endings
Land, don't summarize (except where the form demands a recap, like a brief or
abstract). Effective closes: return to an image from the open with new meaning; a
reframe that recasts what came before; a concrete stake; a clean, declarative line.
Avoid: "In conclusion," "At the end of the day," uplift platitudes, "only time will
tell," and restating the intro.

## Rhythm
Vary sentence length deliberately. A short sentence after two long ones lands like
a punch. Read it aloud in your head; if every sentence is the same length, the prose
drones. Avoid the staccato stack (three+ short sentences in a row) unless you want
that exact effect. Paragraphs can vary too: a one-line paragraph carries weight
precisely because it's rare.

## Voice-matching (ghostwriting)
When matching a sample:
1. Read it for diction (formal/casual, latinate/plain), sentence length and
   variation, punctuation habits, favorite connectives, and degree of hedging.
2. Note signature moves and recurring metaphor domains.
3. Draft, then check yours against theirs on those same axes.
4. The de-slop gate runs in service of *their* voice. Don't normalize their
   quirks into generic clean prose. Preserve the idiosyncrasy that makes it theirs.

## Specificity
The single highest-leverage edit: replace the general with the particular. "A
significant improvement" → "cut load time from 4s to 900ms." "Various
stakeholders" → name them. "Recently" → the date. Abstractions are where AI prose
goes to die; concrete nouns and verbs are where human prose lives.

## Cutting
First drafts are 20 to 40% too long. On the tightening pass: delete every word that
doesn't change meaning, every sentence that repeats a prior one, every paragraph
that doesn't advance the piece. "Omit needless words" is still the rule. If a
sentence survives only because it's pretty, it goes.

## Showing vs. telling (beyond fiction)
Even in nonfiction, demonstrate rather than assert. Instead of "the process was
inefficient," show the seven approvals a single request needed. Evidence persuades;
adjectives don't.
