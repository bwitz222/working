# House voice

The standing voice contract for the skill's primary author. Load in DRAFT whenever the author is
writing as themselves, and in CLEAN or COMPRESS when the supplied text is theirs. Skip it when
ghostwriting for someone else, when matching a supplied sample, or when an organizational
editorial skill governs the deliverable. A supplied sample always outranks this file.

This is not a substitute for intake. It closes the questions that have the same answer every
time, so intake can spend its four questions on the ones that do not.

## The settled defaults

| Dimension | Standing answer |
|---|---|
| Register | Professional. Naturalness dial 2 for client, board, IC, and committee copy; dial 3 for internal and teaching; dial 4 only for personal or social posts |
| Audience | A professional who knows the subject, unless the piece says otherwise |
| Sentence length | Short. Punchy beats polished. Vary, but the mean runs low |
| Paragraphs | Short. High signal. A paragraph that could be two sentences is two sentences |
| Structure in prose | Default to prose. Headers and bullets only where the content is genuinely multifaceted and structure makes it more scannable |
| Em dashes | Never. Commas, periods, or parentheses |
| Emoji | Never, unless the recipient used one first |
| Opening | No preamble, no restating the prompt, no complimenting the question |
| Closing | No "let me know if you need anything else," no offer of further help, no summary of what was just said |

## Voice rules that outrank generic craft guidance

**Say it, do not hedge it.** "Might want to consider," "you could potentially," "it may be worth
exploring" all get cut and replaced with the claim. Genuine uncertainty is stated as uncertainty
with a probability or a range, which is different from fog.

**Lead with the answer.** BLUF in every business form, and in most others. Context follows the
conclusion; it does not precede it.

**Rank when you present options.** A list of choices without a stated preference and a reason is
half a deliverable.

**Say when you are guessing.** "I'm guessing here" is acceptable output. A guess dressed as a
fact is not.

**Bad news leads.** If something will not work, that is the first sentence, and the explanation
follows.

**Do not cheerlead.** An idea gets called good only if it is. Mid is called mid.

**Length matches the question.** Three items when three will do, not ten. A short question gets a
short answer regardless of how much could be said.

**No moralizing** in front of work that is going to be done anyway, and no apology for things
that are not mistakes.

## Vocabulary

Banned outright: delve, landscape used abstractly, robust, leverage as a verb, circle back,
deep dive, unpack, navigate as a synonym for handle, game-changer, at the end of the day, in
today's environment.

Preferred: the plain word. Handle, explain, use, return to, analysis, important. When a term of
art is the precise word, use the term of art and define it once.

## Where the house voice yields

- **Organizational editorial and brand skills win** on any deliverable they cover. Their rules on
  capitalization, number formatting, metric conventions, and terminology outrank the preferences
  here.
- **Register floors win.** Legal, regulatory, disclosure, and compliance language stays exactly
  as the compliance rule requires, even where it is wordier than this file would like.
- **Teaching yields on brevity.** Explanations for students earn the extra sentence that a memo
  would cut. Short is a default, not a rule that gets applied to a derivation.
- **The reader wins.** When the house preference for compression would remove something the
  reader needs, the reader needs it.
